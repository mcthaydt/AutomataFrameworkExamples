## `most` = 16

Sound. The plan's rule was "the heaviest weight at which the hero still pushes the crate onto the plate within ten
seconds: try 4, then lower by 0.5". It assumed 4 was near the edge; measured, it is not (4 kg opens the gate 6 ticks
after 1 kg). Read literally, "the heaviest within ten seconds" is 18 (350 ticks, 5.7 s); 18.5 is already past it
(707 ticks). 16 opens at 161 ticks (2.5 s from the hero's start, about twice the default), well before the cliff
between 17 and 18.5, so the slider is felt at both ends and a small physics change will not push the heaviest crate
past ten seconds. The choice is disclosed with its numbers and is one value in `crate.json`. Note N1 records that it
departs from the plan's wording.

## The heavy parity test

Meaningful:

- It plays the real level 1 (`loadTheGame`) with only the crate's `values.heavy` changed, so it tests the shipped content.
- On the old pin the weight is ignored, so every weight opens on the same tick; "later at the heaviest", "never sooner
  as the weight goes up" and "more than 60 ticks slower" would all fail. It cannot pass without F4 working.
- It runs in Node and in all three browser projects (`*.parity.test.ts`), saves the heaviest run's hashes as
  `.hashes/<engine>.heavy.json`, and `scripts/same-hash.ts` now compares `heavy` too. The play stops on the tick the
  gate opens, so an engine that opens on another tick ends on another hash and fails the comparison. A missing file
  fails too ("did not report its hashes").
